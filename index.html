01 / ABOUT

Where AI governance meets security engineering.

AI risk can no longer be treated as a policy problem alone. Effective governance requires standards, technical controls, threat-informed design, rigorous assurance and continuous testing.

IAIGSRM brings these disciplines together in an independent institute model focused on research, education and practical engagement with public, private and non-profit organizations.

04 / RESEARCH & PUBLICATIONS

Research intended to become practice.

IAIGSRM develops applied research at the intersection of AI governance, cybersecurity, risk, ethics and assurance, emphasizing methods organizations can implement and evaluate.

Developing work includes an IAIGSRM AI Threat & Mitigating Control Framework: Tier 1 threat and risk model, Tier 2 minimum mitigating controls, and Tier 3 high-assurance controls for sensitive, regulated and high-security environments.

AI Regulation & Policy

European Union Artificial Intelligence Act

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 establishes a harmonized, risk-based framework for artificial intelligence in the European Union, including prohibited practices, high-risk systems, transparency requirements, general-purpose AI, governance and enforcement.

02

History & Evolution of AI

1950 — Alan TuringMachine intelligence and the question that became the Turing Test.

1956 — DartmouthAI emerges as a distinct research field; John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky and other pioneers shape its early agenda.

1950s–1970s — Symbolic AIReasoning, search, knowledge representation and problem solving.

1970s–1980s — Expert Systems & KBSApplied narrow AI using specialist knowledge, rules, inference engines and structured knowledge bases.

AI WintersFunding and expectations contract as early approaches encounter practical limits.

1990s–2000s — Machine LearningIncreasing emphasis on statistical and data-driven learning.

2010s — Deep Learning & DeepMindNeural networks, large-scale computation and milestones including AlphaGo accelerate modern AI.

2017–Present — Transformers & LLMsTransformer architectures enable large-scale generative and multimodal systems.

Emerging — Agentic AISystems increasingly plan and act through tools, APIs, memory and external environments.

Frontier — AGI & SuperintelligenceResearch into increasingly general capabilities raises new governance, security, assurance and human-oversight questions.

06 / FOUNDER

Stephen Buerle, PhD, CISSP

Founder, Executive Director & Head of Research

His career has evolved from early work as an ethical hacker, maker, systems analyst and developer into applied artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, AI governance, controls development, assurance and risk management. His cybersecurity experience has spanned penetration testing, cyber defense, security monitoring and incident response, as well as security architecture and engineering.

His early work in artificial intelligence included the development and application of expert systems and knowledge-based systems, followed by academic research involving machine learning. His subsequent work has brought these disciplines together through enterprise cybersecurity, AI governance, security architecture, control design and assessment, and the development and evaluation of applied AI systems.

His professional experience includes work with the International Atomic Energy Agency, Unisys, JPMorgan Chase, IBM and Kyndryl. His academic background includes degrees from Harvard University, Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute and the University at Albany, SUNY.
